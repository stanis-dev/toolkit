# Meeting ASR for Brain: Whisper large-v3 versus 2025-2026 alternatives

Research date: 2026-09-04. Scope: local speech-to-text for 15 to 90 minute meeting recordings, 2 to 8 speakers, mostly Spanish with English terms mixed in, on an M4 Max with 64 GB. Requirements: accurate text, word-level timestamps for speaker assignment, automatic language detection, no translation. Sources are model cards, official repos, vendor posts and the Open ASR Leaderboard result files. Leaderboard numbers below come from the CSVs the leaderboard app loads (`hf-audio/multilingual_evals/multilingual_es.csv`, last modified 2026-08-28; `hf-audio/leaderboard_longform/longform_latest.csv`, 2026-07-10; `hf-audio/open-asr-leaderboard-results/english_short_latest.csv`, 2026-08-28). The Spanish average is my arithmetic mean of the three per-dataset columns; the app computes the same.

## 1. Verdict

1. No local open-weight model beats Whisper large-v3 on Spanish while also providing word timestamps and automatic language detection. Keep large-v3 as Brain's transcription model.
2. On the leaderboard's Spanish track only three open models average lower WER than large-v3 (3.35): Cohere Transcribe 03-2026 at 2.81 (no timestamps, no language detection, 35 s windows), Voxtral Small 24B at 2.91 (no word timestamps, 24B params) and Canary-1B-v2 at 3.23 (native word timestamps, source language must be given). The gap is 0.1 to 0.5 WER points on read speech, and Whisper still wins FLEURS Spanish outright (2.30).
3. The measurable upgrade is the aligner, not the recognizer: Qwen3-ForcedAligner-0.6B (released 2026-01-28, Apache 2.0, Spanish and English among 11 languages, runs in mlx-audio) reports a 36.8 ms average word-boundary shift on Spanish against 108.0 ms for the WhisperX wav2vec2 aligner that whispermlx uses, and 39.6 ms against 4549.9 ms on 300 s concatenations. Word-to-speaker assignment is the stated reason Brain needs word timestamps.
4. English is where large-v3 lags: 5.78 on the English track against 4.31 for Qwen3-ASR-1.7B, 4.67 for Cohere Transcribe and 4.86 for Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3. Qwen3-ASR-1.7B is the only one of those with automatic language detection plus a Spanish-capable word aligner, and its Spanish average is 3.80, so it only pays off for English-heavy meetings.
5. Two candidates deserve a benchmark on real Brain recordings before any switch: Canary-1B-v2 through mlx-audio (word timestamps, 1466 RTFx on GPU, Spanish 3.23) and Apple's on-device SpeechTranscriber (macOS 26; es_ES, es_MX, es_US and es_CL locales downloadable on this Mac; word timing; vocabulary biasing; no published WER; a locale must be chosen up front). VibeVoice-ASR is the only model claiming built-in diarization plus native code-switching, and its Spanish Common Voice WER of 11.87 rules it out today.

## 3. Per-candidate notes

### Whisper large-v3 and variants

Whisper large-v3 (https://huggingface.co/openai/whisper-large-v3, created 2023-11-07, Apache 2.0) sits at 3.35 on the Spanish track with the best FLEURS Spanish number of any open model (2.30) and a weak Common Voice number (4.29). On the English track it is rank 45 of 61 at 5.78 with AMI 13.63, the worst AMI of any model in the top 50 (`english_short_latest.csv`). On the long-form English track it scores 11.23 with CORAAL and 8.67 without, which places it ahead of Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3 (9.10 without CORAAL) and behind Cohere Transcribe (7.86) (`longform_latest.csv`).

large-v3-turbo (https://huggingface.co/openai/whisper-large-v3-turbo, created 2024-10-01, MIT, 809M params, 4 decoder layers) trades 0.5 Spanish WER points (3.85 vs 3.35) for about 1.7x throughput on the leaderboard (RTFx 792 vs 470 on the English track). The OpenAI CHANGELOG (https://github.com/openai/whisper/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md) lists v20240930 for turbo and v20250625 as the latest release with fixes only. The openai Hugging Face organization has no speech model newer than turbo (HF API, models by author `openai`, sorted by createdAt), so there is no 2026 open-weight OpenAI speech model.

CrisperWhisper 2.0 (https://huggingface.co/nyralabs/CrisperWhisper2.0_large, created 2026-07-15, 1.54B params) is a Whisper-based verbatim transcriber with supervised cross-attention word alignment (29.6 ms mean boundary error on TIMIT, 41 ms on conversational speech), conditional-continuation long-form decoding and a CTranslate2 runtime with speculative decoding. The card says verbatim and intended modes "work across most languages Whisper supports" and reports a 10-language disfluency benchmark in which only English and German use human-labeled sets. It publishes no Spanish WER. The license is the Nyra Health Non-Commercial Research License; commercial use needs a separate license; the Pro models with hotword boosting are commercial-only. No MLX or CoreML runtime exists. The 2024 CrisperWhisper (https://huggingface.co/nyrahealth/CrisperWhisper) is English and German only, CC-BY-NC-4.0.

distil-large-v3.5 (https://huggingface.co/distil-whisper/distil-large-v3.5) is English only. The multilingual distillations by the same author, whisper-large-v3-distil-multi7-v0.2 (https://huggingface.co/bofenghuang/whisper-large-v3-distil-multi7-v0.2, MIT, 756M, 2 decoder layers, en/fr/es/de/it/pt/nl), score worse than large-v3 on every Spanish set in the card table (mcv17 5.70 vs 4.91, mls 4.35 vs 3.97, fleurs 5.15 vs 4.22; voxpopuli is the exception at 8.55 vs 11.06). The card says the model repurposes the `<|yue|>` token as an automatic language-detection token and emits a new language token when it detects a language change inside a segment, which is the only Whisper-family code-switching mechanism I found with published weights.

### NVIDIA

Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3 (https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3, created 2025-08-04, CC-BY-4.0, 627M params, FastConformer-TDT) covers 25 European languages including Spanish, detects the language automatically, and outputs punctuation, capitalization and word plus segment timestamps natively. The card states "supporting audio up to 24 minutes long with full attention (on A100 80GB) or up to 3 hours with local attention" and gives Spanish WER of 3.45 (FLEURS es_419), 4.39 (MLS) and 3.41 (CoVoST2). On the leaderboard Spanish track it averages 3.71 (FLEURS 3.25, MCV 3.53, MLS 4.36) at RTFx 3786, so it beats large-v3 on Common Voice audio and loses on FLEURS and MLS. English 4.86, long-form 10.72. The card does not say whether a single utterance may switch language. Apple Silicon: parakeet-mlx (default model `mlx-community/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3`, chunking 120 s with 15 s overlap or local attention, word-level `AlignedToken` timestamps; https://github.com/senstella/parakeet-mlx) and FluidAudio (CoreML on the Neural Engine, 14.96 s windows with 2 s overlap, "~120x on M4 Pro (1 minute = 0.5 seconds)" in Documentation/ASR/GettingStarted.md and "~190x on M4 Pro" in the README; https://github.com/FluidInference/FluidAudio). Memory: 627M params, about 1.3 GB in bf16, 2.5 GB in fp32.

Canary-1B-v2 (https://huggingface.co/nvidia/canary-1b-v2, created 2025-08-04, CC-BY-4.0, 979M params, FastConformer encoder plus 8-layer Transformer decoder) covers the same 25 languages, gives word and segment timestamps for transcription, and uses dynamic chunking with 1 s overlap for audio above 40 s. Source and target languages are passed explicitly; the card documents no automatic language detection. Card metadata lists FLEURS Spanish WER 2.90; the card body reports Earnings-22 long-form 13.78 and This American Life 9.87. Leaderboard Spanish 3.23 (FLEURS 2.63, MCV 3.94, MLS 3.13) at RTFx 1466, English 5.71 (AMI 13.03). Apple Silicon: mlx-audio's `canary` model loads community conversions (`qfuxa/canary-mlx`, `Mediform/canary-1b-v2-mlx-q8`); NVIDIA's `.nemo` weights need conversion (https://github.com/Blaizzy/mlx-audio/blob/main/mlx_audio/stt/models/canary/README.md).

Canary-Qwen-2.5B (https://huggingface.co/nvidia/canary-qwen-2.5b, 2025-06-26) is English only, trained on 40 s maximum, no timestamps. Not applicable.

Nemotron 3.5 ASR streaming 0.6B (https://huggingface.co/nvidia/nemotron-3.5-asr-streaming-0.6b, created 2026-05-15, OpenMDW-1.1 license, 638M params) is the newest NVIDIA ASR release. It is a cache-aware streaming FastConformer-RNNT covering 40 language-locales with Spanish (es-US, es-ES) in the "transcription-ready" tier, language-ID prompt conditioning and a `target_lang=auto` mode. Card FLEURS Spanish: 4.11 at 1.12 s look-ahead, 4.87 at 80 ms. Leaderboard Spanish 5.43, English 7.88. Word timestamps are not documented. It runs in mlx-audio (`mlx-community/nemotron-3.5-asr-streaming-0.6b`, 1.2 GB bf16) and FluidAudio. It is a streaming model and scores below Whisper offline. NVIDIA's other 2026 ASR repos are `parakeet-unified-en-0.6b` (2026-04-07, English) and `parakeet-ctc-0.6b-Vietnamese` (HF API listing).

### Mistral Voxtral

Mistral's 2026 speech posts are "Voxtral transcribes at the speed of sound" (2026-02-04, https://mistral.ai/news/voxtral-transcribe-2/) and the Voxtral TTS post (2026-03-23). Voxtral Transcribe 2 is two models. Voxtral Mini Transcribe V2 is the batch model with speaker diarization, word-level timestamps, context biasing of up to 100 words ("optimized for English; support for other languages is experimental"), 13 languages including Spanish and 3 h per request; it is API-only at USD 0.003 per minute (the mistralai HF organization holds no Transcribe V2 weights; HF API listing 2026-09-04). Its FLEURS Spanish WER is 2.63 in the table on the Realtime card. Voxtral Mini 4B Realtime 2602 (https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Voxtral-Mini-4B-Realtime-2602, created 2026-01-21, Apache 2.0, 4.43B params, 13 languages) is the open model: a natively streaming architecture with configurable delay, FLEURS Spanish 3.31 at 480 ms and 2.71 at 2400 ms (card table), a default context of 131072 tokens ("~ca. 3h"), 16 GB GPU recommended. The card does not mention word timestamps or diarization. Leaderboard Spanish 4.18, English 6.46. Apple Silicon: mlx-audio `voxtral_realtime` and `mlx-community/Voxtral-Mini-4B-Realtime-2602-fp16` (created 2026-02-06); the card lists MLX among untested community ports.

Voxtral Mini 3B 2507 (https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Voxtral-Mini-3B-2507, 2025-07-01, Apache 2.0, 4.68B params, 8 languages including Spanish) transcribes up to 30 min per request, detects the language automatically, needs about 9.5 GB in bf16, and has no word timestamps. Leaderboard Spanish 4.16. Voxtral Small 24B 2507 (https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Voxtral-Small-24B-2507, 24.3B params) is the second-best open model on the Spanish track at 2.91 (FLEURS 2.79, MCV 2.90, MLS 3.05) with RTFx 101 and English 4.99 (AMI 13.19); it has no word timestamps and needs about 48 GB in bf16 or roughly 13 GB at 4-bit. I did not verify the Small model's transcription length limit or its mlx-audio support.

### Qwen3-ASR

Qwen3-ASR (https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-ASR, https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-ASR-1.7B, created 2026-01-28, Apache 2.0; native Transformers support added 2026-06-26 as `Qwen/Qwen3-ASR-1.7B-hf`) ships 1.7B and 0.6B models covering 30 languages including Spanish and English plus 22 Chinese dialects, with built-in language identification (98.7% on FLEURS for 1.7B) and a single model for streaming and offline use. The README says the models "support transcribe long audio" and gives no maximum; the `-hf` card says the same. mlx-audio's implementation chunks long inputs (its README notes `batch_size` "only batches chunks within one input"). Published multilingual averages from the README (Qwen3-ASR-1.7B vs Whisper-large-v3): MLS 8.55 vs 8.62, CommonVoice 9.18 vs 10.77, FLEURS 4.90 vs 5.27; no Spanish-only row exists. Leaderboard Spanish 3.80 (FLEURS 2.92, MCV 4.03, MLS 4.46), so it beats large-v3 only on the Common Voice column. English 4.31, rank 7 overall and the best open model on that track. Apple Silicon: mlx-audio `qwen3_asr` with `mlx-community/Qwen3-ASR-1.7B-8bit` and `-bf16` (created 2026-01-29). Stored size 2.35B params, about 4.7 GB bf16.

Qwen3-ForcedAligner-0.6B (https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-ForcedAligner-0.6B, created 2026-01-28, Apache 2.0, 918M stored params) is a non-autoregressive aligner for Chinese, English, Cantonese, French, German, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese, Russian and Spanish, up to 5 minutes of speech per call, word or character granularity. The README table (Accumulated Average Shift in ms, lower is better) gives Spanish 36.8 vs WhisperX 108.0 and NFA 124.7 on MFA-labeled raw data, and Spanish 39.6 vs WhisperX 4549.9 on 300 s concatenations; the WhisperX aligner is the wav2vec2 pipeline whispermlx inherits. It runs in mlx-audio as `mlx-community/Qwen3-ForcedAligner-0.6B-8bit`, taking audio plus a transcript and returning per-word start and end times. It aligns text from any recognizer, so it can replace the alignment stage while Whisper stays.

### Cohere Transcribe

Cohere Transcribe 03-2026 (https://huggingface.co/CohereLabs/cohere-transcribe-03-2026, created 2026-03-24, Apache 2.0, 2.07B params, Conformer encoder plus 8-layer Transformer decoder; release post https://huggingface.co/blog/CohereLabs/cohere-transcribe-03-2026-release) is the best open model on the Spanish track at 2.81 (FLEURS 3.26, MCV 2.79, MLS 2.38) and on the English long-form track at 9.73 (7.86 without CORAAL), with English short-form 4.67. It covers 14 languages including Spanish and English. The card states the model "does not feature" timestamps or speaker diarization and "does not feature explicit, automatic language detection"; the language is passed in the prompt. Audio above `max_audio_clip_s` is split automatically; FluidAudio's port documents the cap as 35 s per call with 5 s overlap when chunking. The release post notes the model "is eager to transcribe, even non-speech sounds" and recommends a VAD in front. Apple Silicon: mlx-audio `cohere_asr` (loads the CohereLabs repo directly) and FluidAudio (INT8 encoder 1.8 GB plus FP32 decoder). About 4 GB in bf16. A second model, cohere-transcribe-arabic-07-2026, appeared 2026-06-18.

### IBM Granite Speech

Granite Speech 4.1 2B (https://huggingface.co/ibm-granite/granite-speech-4.1-2b, created 2026-04-16, Apache 2.0, 2.31B params) covers English, French, German, Spanish, Portuguese and Japanese, uses 4 s block attention, and states no maximum audio length. English 4.62 (AMI 7.06). The base model has no timestamps or language detection. The Spanish track lists only the non-autoregressive variant granite-speech-4.1-2b-nar at 3.63 (FLEURS 3.61, MCV 3.95, MLS 3.34, RTFx 1618). Granite Speech 4.1 2B Plus (https://huggingface.co/ibm-granite/granite-speech-4.1-2b-plus, 2.11B params, en/fr/de/es/pt) adds speaker-attributed ASR (`[Speaker N]:` tags in order of appearance) and word-level `[T:N]` end-time tags in centiseconds modulo 1000, which roll over every 10 s; the card says the model "works well with audio segments up to 9 minutes long for ASR and SAA, and up to 3.5 minutes for timestamps" and that the plus mode drops punctuation and capitalization. Reported metrics: WDER 0.9% on Fisher, AAS 11.4 ms on LibriSpeech clean. Runtimes listed: Transformers 5.8+, vLLM 0.23+. mlx-audio supports `granite_speech` (the 4.1 base card lists mlx-audio 0.4.1+). Granite Speech 3.3 8B (2025-04-14) is superseded on every track by 4.1 2B. Granite Speech 5.0 470M TurboCTC (2026-08-04) is English only.

### Kyutai, Omnilingual ASR, VibeVoice

Kyutai STT (https://huggingface.co/kyutai/stt-2.6b-en, created 2025-06-06, CC-BY-4.0) is English only; the 1B sibling is English and French. Kyutai released no multilingual STT in 2026 (HF API listing). Not applicable.

Omnilingual ASR (https://github.com/facebookresearch/omnilingual-asr, models created 2025-11-27, v2 "Unlimited" variants added December 2025, Apache 2.0) targets 1600+ languages; omniASR-LLM-7B-v2 scores 3.38 on the Spanish track (FLEURS 2.93, MCV 4.00, MLS 3.20) at RTFx 53, level with Whisper. The standard models cap at 40 s; the unlimited v2 variants remove the limit. The 7B card lists about 17 GiB VRAM in bf16 and fairseq2 as the runtime. No timestamps and no Apple Silicon runtime are documented.

VibeVoice-ASR (https://huggingface.co/microsoft/VibeVoice-ASR, created 2026-01-21, MIT, 9B params; docs https://github.com/microsoft/VibeVoice/blob/main/docs/vibevoice-asr.md) is a single model that takes up to 60 minutes of audio in one pass within 64K tokens and emits speaker, timestamps and text, accepts hotwords, "requires no explicit language setting, and natively handles code-switching within and across utterances" across 50+ languages including Spanish. The docs give MLC-Challenge Spanish DER 2.67, cpWER 10.51, tcpWER 11.71, WER 8.04, and AMI-SDM English DER 13.43, WER 24.65. On the leaderboard it averages 6.68 on Spanish (FLEURS 3.58, MCV 11.87, MLS 4.60) and 5.58 on English at RTFx 136. Timestamp granularity is not stated in the card or docs. Apple Silicon: mlx-audio `vibevoice_asr`; the bf16 checkpoint is about 17 GB. VibeVoice-ASR-Streaming 1.5B and 7B (created 2026-09-02, MIT, 10 languages including Spanish) emit speaker and content without timestamps per the mlx-audio table.

### Cloud reference: ElevenLabs Scribe v2

Scribe v2 (https://elevenlabs.io/blog/introducing-scribe-v2, 2026-01-09) is cloud-only and leads the leaderboard English short-form track (3.97) and long-form track (7.32); its Spanish average is 2.32 (FLEURS 1.85, MCV 2.55, MLS 2.55), second to Azure Speech 06-2026 (2.25). The post lists word-level timestamps and speaker diarization and publishes no numeric WER. Mistral's Transcribe 2 post claims about 3x the speed of Scribe v2 at one fifth the price.

### Apple SpeechAnalyzer and SpeechTranscriber (macOS 26)

Apple's Speech framework gained `SpeechAnalyzer` and `SpeechTranscriber` in macOS 26 (https://developer.apple.com/documentation/speech/speechanalyzer, availability macOS 26.0). The WWDC25 session (https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/277/) states "transcription is entirely on device but the models need to be fetched", that the model "is good for long-form and distant audio, such as lectures, meetings, and conversations", and that "Each run has an audioTimeRange attribute represented as CMTimeRange". The macOS 26 SDK interface (`Speech.swiftmodule/arm64e-apple-macos.swiftinterface` in Xcode on this machine) shows `SpeechTranscriber.init(locale:preset:)`, presets including `timeIndexedTranscriptionWithAlternatives`, `ResultAttributeOption.audioTimeRange`, `SpeechAnalyzer.init(inputAudioFile:modules:)` for file input, `AnalysisContext.contextualStrings` for vocabulary biasing, and a `SpeechDetector` module for voice activity only; there is no language-identification API, so the locale is fixed per transcriber. Running `SpeechTranscriber.supportedLocales` on this Mac (macOS 26.6.2) returned 30 locales including es_CL, es_ES, es_MX and es_US; `installedLocales` returned only the nine English locales, so a Spanish asset download is needed once. Apple publishes no WER.

### Code-switching (Spanish with English terms)

No model card among the candidates reports a Spanish-English code-switching benchmark. Whisper decodes one language token per 30 s window, so an English term inside Spanish speech is transcribed under the Spanish token. The CUI 2024 paper "¿Te vienes? Sure!" (Hillah, Dubiel, Leiva, https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3640794.3665579) fine-tunes Whisper-Large-V2 on the Bangor Miami corpus (23.14 h after processing) and reports WER between 44.17 and 75.81 across its variants, with the best code-switching result at 46.83 (Spanish-dominant) and 48.38 (English-dominant) when an auxiliary language-identification loss is added; it cites NVIDIA's concatenated-tokenizer work at 50 to 53 WER on the same corpus. These numbers are for conversational bilingual Miami speech and show how far every Whisper variant is from monolingual accuracy on true code-switching. Two models claim in-segment language switching: whisper-large-v3-distil-multi7-v0.2 (new language token on switch, Spanish accuracy below large-v3) and VibeVoice-ASR (native code-switching, Spanish Common Voice 11.87). Brain's current mitigation, an `initial_prompt` carrying English vocabulary, is the only biasing mechanism Whisper offers; Apple's `contextualStrings`, Parakeet's CTC keyword boosting in FluidAudio, VibeVoice hotwords and Mistral's context biasing (English-optimized) are the equivalents elsewhere.

## 4. Runtimes and maintenance

whispermlx (https://github.com/KalebJS/whispermlx, PyPI `whispermlx`): version 3.13.1 uploaded 2026-08-07; 14 releases since the first PyPI release 3.9.0 on 2026-03-23; last push 2026-08-17 (a README parity table); 64 stars; BSD-2-Clause; author Kaleb Smith, with one external PR merged in August fixing MLX cache growth in the transcription loop. It tracks upstream WhisperX's API (upstream is at 3.8.6, 2026-05-25, and 3.8.7rc1, 2026-06-26) with the recognizer swapped to mlx-whisper. Its own parity table states: batched inference is absent ("per-segment", `batch_size` accepted but unused), `asr_options` uses only `initial_prompt`, `compute_type` is ignored, VAD and wav2vec2 alignment and pyannote diarization are unchanged from WhisperX. Dependencies: mlx-whisper 0.4.0+, torch 2.8, pyannote-audio 4.0+, transformers 4.48+, Python 3.10 to 3.13. Brain's `requirements.txt` pins whispermlx 3.13.1 and mlx-whisper 0.4.3; `scripts/transcribe.py` calls `load_model("large-v3", asr_options={"initial_prompt": ...})`, then `align`, then `DiarizationPipeline`.

mlx-whisper (https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-examples/tree/main/whisper, PyPI `mlx-whisper`): 0.4.3 uploaded 2025-08-29; last Whisper commit in mlx-examples 2025-12-15 ("Use model.safetensors with Whisper"); MIT. Word timestamps via `word_timestamps=True` (DTW on cross-attention). Sequential 30 s decoding, no batching. Working but slow-moving.

mlx-audio (https://github.com/Blaizzy/mlx-audio, PyPI `mlx-audio`): 0.5.1 released 2026-08-31, eight releases since 2026-04-28, last push 2026-09-03, 7831 stars, MIT. Its `stt/models` directory contains whisper (segment and DTW word timestamps), parakeet, canary, qwen3_asr, qwen3_forced_aligner, cohere_asr, voxtral, voxtral_realtime, granite_speech, nemotron_asr, vibevoice_asr, moss_transcribe_diarize, wav2vec, mms, sensevoice and others. It is the one Python runtime that covers Whisper and every alternative in this report, which makes side-by-side benchmarking on Brain's recordings a matter of swapping model ids.

parakeet-mlx (https://github.com/senstella/parakeet-mlx, PyPI `parakeet-mlx`): 0.5.2 uploaded 2026-06-05, last push 2026-08-29, 976 stars, Apache 2.0. Word-level timestamps, chunked long audio (120 s chunks, 15 s overlap by default) or local attention without chunking, streaming with configurable context. No speed claims in the README.

lightning-whisper-mlx (https://github.com/mustafaaljadery/lightning-whisper-mlx): last commit 2024-05-08, PyPI 0.0.10 from 2024-04-02, no license file. Abandoned.

WhisperKit, now the Argmax Open-Source SDK (https://github.com/argmaxinc/WhisperKit, redirects to argmax-oss-swift): v1.0.0 on 2026-05-01 renamed the package and CLI (`argmax-cli`) and bundled WhisperKit, SpeakerKit (pyannote-based diarization with speaker centroid embeddings) and TTSKit; v1.1.0 on 2026-08-06 added incremental file loading (120 s VAD-aligned chunks, "70%+ savings for 3-hour audio input") and fixed word-timestamp bugs; last push 2026-08-13; MIT; Swift 6, macOS 14+. Models are Whisper only (recommended `large-v3-v20240930_626MB`, plus turbo); no Parakeet or other architectures in the OSS SDK. Argmax's benchmark space data (https://huggingface.co/spaces/argmaxinc/whisperkit-benchmarks, `dashboard_data/performance_data.json`) has rows for the base Apple M4 on macOS 15.4 (2025-04): large-v3-v20240930 speed 9.19x real time, turbo 14.42x; and Apple M4 Pro on macOS 15.7 (2025-09): turbo 16.77x. No M4 Max rows exist in the file. Brain's current whispermlx run of a 30-minute file in about 60 s is roughly 30x real time on an M4 Max.

FluidAudio (https://github.com/FluidInference/FluidAudio): v0.15.6 on 2026-08-19, last push 2026-09-04, 2730 stars, Apache 2.0, Swift 6, CoreML on the Neural Engine. ASR: Parakeet TDT v3 default (25 languages), Parakeet TDT v2, Cohere Transcribe (35 s cap), Nemotron streaming multilingual with auto language detection, SenseVoice; diarization: Sortformer (4 speakers), LS-EEND (10 speakers), pyannote community-1 offline pipeline; Silero VAD. Long audio runs through `SlidingWindowAsrManager` with 14.96 s windows and 2 s overlap. Word timestamps come from the TDT decoder (Documentation/ASR/CustomVocabulary.md). Speed claims: "~120x on M4 Pro" (GettingStarted.md) and "~190x on M4 Pro" (README). This is the Swift-native path to Parakeet or Cohere without Python.

Apple Speech framework: ships with macOS 26, no third-party dependency, Swift API, model managed by the OS (`AssetInventory.assetInstallationRequest`). Locale fixed per transcriber; `contextualStrings` for vocabulary; `audioTimeRange` per run.

## 5. What I could not verify

- Whether Voxtral Mini 4B Realtime emits word timestamps: the model card does not mention timestamps or diarization, and the mlx-audio `voxtral_realtime` directory has no README.
- VibeVoice-ASR timestamp granularity (word versus speaker turn): neither the card nor the docs state it.
- Voxtral Small 24B's transcription length limit and its mlx-audio support: I verified only the Mini 3B card (30 min) and the presence of a `voxtral` model directory in mlx-audio.
- Qwen3-ASR's maximum input duration in Transformers: the README and the `-hf` card say "long audio" without a number.
- Per-language multilingual WER for Cohere Transcribe and Voxtral Mini 3B from their own cards: both publish charts, not tables. The leaderboard CSV supplies the Spanish numbers used here.
- ElevenLabs Scribe v2 and Azure Speech 06-2026 Spanish numbers exist only in the leaderboard CSV; the vendors' posts publish no per-language WER.
- Apple SpeechTranscriber accuracy and speed: Apple publishes neither. The locale list above was read on this machine; installing a Spanish asset and measuring WER on a Brain recording is the only way to place it.
- Whisper large-v3 zero-shot WER on Spanish-English code-switched speech: the CUI 2024 paper reports fine-tuned Large-V2 variants (44 to 76 WER) and describes zero-shot as "somewhat competitive" without a table row.
- Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3 behaviour when a single utterance switches language: the card describes per-audio automatic language detection only.
- Cohere Transcribe's leaderboard English number changed from 5.42 (card, March 2026) to 4.67 (current CSV) because the leaderboard moved to cleaned dataset variants; the two figures are not comparable.
- WhisperKit speed on an M4 Max: the benchmark file has M4 and M4 Pro rows only.
- Real meeting-audio accuracy for any candidate: the Spanish track uses read speech (FLEURS, Common Voice, MLS audiobooks); the long-form track is English only (earnings calls, TED, CORAAL interviews). No public benchmark matches Spanish multi-speaker meetings.
