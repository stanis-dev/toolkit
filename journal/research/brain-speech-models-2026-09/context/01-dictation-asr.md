# Dictation ASR for Brain: is there a better on-device model than Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3? (checked 2026-09-04)

Use case: hotkey dictation on an M4 Max, macOS 26.6.2, 5 s to 2 min of mostly Spanish with English technical terms, transcribed from a wav after key release. Latency after release first, accuracy second. Current: `mlx-community/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3` via `parakeet-mlx` 0.5.2, one Python process spawned per dictation (`brain/scripts/dictate.py`).

## Verdict

1. Keep Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3 as the default. No open model released since June 2026, and none missed earlier, beats it on both speed and Spanish accuracy; on the Open ASR Leaderboard Spanish track it is the fastest entry (RTFx 3786) and within 0.6 WER points of the best local open model on FLEURS es (3.25 vs 2.63 for Canary-1B-v2) while beating that model on Common Voice es (3.53 vs 3.94) and on English (4.86 vs 5.71).
2. The one open model that is more accurate than Parakeet v3 on both English and Spanish FLEURS is Qwen3-ASR-1.7B (released 2026-01-29, Apache 2.0): English avg WER 4.31 vs 4.86, FLEURS es 2.92 vs 3.25, but Common Voice es 4.03 vs 3.53 and MLS es 4.46 vs 4.36. It runs in MLX today (mlx-community 4 to 8 bit, `mlx-audio`, `mlx-qwen3-asr`) with word timestamps through Qwen3-ForcedAligner-0.6B (Spanish included), and on the local code-switched test clip it was the only model with a perfect transcript. It is disqualified by latency: 3.8 to 4.4 s to transcribe an 18.7 s clip on this M4 Max (Parakeet: 0.20 to 0.72 s), so a 2 min dictation would wait about 25 s. The cheap accuracy upgrade to trial instead is whisper-large-v3-turbo through the `mlx-whisper` already in Brain's venv: 0.38 s warm on the same clip, one error where Parakeet made two, better than Parakeet on FLEURS es (2.73 vs 3.25) and MLS es (3.85 vs 4.36), worse on Common Voice es (4.98 vs 3.53) and English (6.36 vs 4.86), and known to hallucinate on silence.
3. Canary-1B-v2 (2025-08-14, CC-BY-4.0) has the best Spanish numbers of any local open model (FLEURS 2.63, MCV 3.94, MLS 3.13) but is worse than Parakeet on English (5.71), has no mlx-community conversion, and its Apple Silicon path is a beta CoreML engine in FluidAudio v0.15.6 (2026-08-19).
4. Apple SpeechAnalyzer (Speech framework, macOS 26.0+) is on-device, needs no Python, supports es-ES, es-MX, es-US and es-CL (queried on this machine), and transcribed an 18.7 s Spanish clip in 0.20 s here. It has no automatic language identification (one locale per transcriber), Apple publishes no WER, the only third-party measurement (Argmax, June 2025, English) puts it at mid-tier Whisper accuracy, and on a code-switched Spanish test clip it garbled nearly every English term that Parakeet got right. Not a replacement for this use case.
5. The largest latency win available is a runtime change, with the same weights: on this Mac the cold Parakeet process costs 1.5 to 2.1 s in total, of which 0.7 to 1.1 s is interpreter start plus model load and only 0.55 to 0.72 s is the first transcription (0.20 s warm). Brain already hides the load behind the user's speech; a Swift-native runtime (FluidAudio CoreML, macOS 14+, Apache 2.0; or `mlx-audio-swift`, MIT, which also carries Qwen3-ASR and Canary) removes the spawn and the first-call warm-up entirely.

## Per-candidate notes

### Apple SpeechAnalyzer / SpeechTranscriber / DictationTranscriber

- API: `SpeechAnalyzer` "Analyzes spoken audio content in various ways and manages the analysis session", macOS 26.0+; file input through `analyzeSequence(from: AVAudioFile)` and `start(inputAudioFile:finishAfterFile:)`; results carry `audioTimeRange` (CMTimeRange) when the `audioTimeRange` attribute option is set. https://developer.apple.com/documentation/speech/speechanalyzer
- `SpeechTranscriber` presets: `transcription` (no volatile results, no time ranges), `timeIndexedTranscriptionWithAlternatives` (time ranges), `progressiveTranscription` (volatile + fast results). https://developer.apple.com/documentation/speech/speechtranscriber/preset
- On-device: WWDC25 session 277: "Transcription is entirely on device but the models need to be fetched" and "The model is retained in system storage and does not increase the download or storage size of your application". https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/277/
- Assets: `AssetInventory.assetInstallationRequest(supporting:)` then `downloadAndInstall()`; "Once assets are downloaded, they persist between app launches and are shared between apps." https://developer.apple.com/documentation/speech/assetinventory
- Locales on this machine (macOS 26.6.2, Xcode 26.6, queried with `SpeechTranscriber.supportedLocales`): 30 locales: de-AT de-CH de-DE en-AU en-CA en-GB en-IE en-IN en-NZ en-SG en-US en-ZA es-CL es-ES es-MX es-US fr-BE fr-CA fr-CH fr-FR it-CH it-IT ja-JP ko-KR pt-BR pt-PT yue-CN zh-CN zh-HK zh-TW. Only the 9 English locales were installed before this test. `DictationTranscriber.supportedLocales` returned 54 locales (adds ar, ca, cs, da, el, fi, he, hi, hr, hu, id, ms, nb, nl, pl, ro, ru, sk, sv, th, tr, uk, vi). Apple does not publish this list in the docs; the WWDC session says "SpeechTranscriber can currently transcribe these languages, with more to come" over a slide.
- `DictationTranscriber` "uses the same speech-to-text machine learning models as system dictation features do, or as SFSpeechRecognizer does when it is configured for on-device operation." https://developer.apple.com/documentation/speech/dictationtranscriber
- No language identification: a transcriber is created for one locale. On the Spanish test clip, an en-US transcriber produced nonsense ("Oh, yes, tube, revisando, el pulrico, del backend"), so a Spanish/English dictation must pick a locale up front.
- Accuracy: Apple publishes no WER. Argmax (vendor of WhisperKit, so an interested party) measured on an M4 Mac mini with macOS 26 beta 1, June 20, 2025, English earnings22 10 percent subset: SpeechAnalyzer WER 14.0 vs WhisperKit small.en 12.8 and base.en 15.2; speed factor 70 vs 111 (base.en) and 35 (small.en); Argmax Pro parakeet-v2 359. Conclusion in their words: "Apple matches the speed and accuracy of mid-tier OpenAI Whisper models on long-form conversational speech transcription." No multilingual numbers. https://www.argmaxinc.com/blog/apple-and-argmax
- macOS 26.1, 26.2, 26.4 and 26.6 developer release notes contain no Speech framework entries (checked https://developer.apple.com/documentation/macos-release-notes/macos-26_1-release-notes and the 26_2, 26_4, 26_6 pages).
- Local test result: see "Local measurements".

### Mistral Voxtral

- Voxtral Transcribe 2 announced 2026-02-04: "Voxtral Mini Transcribe V2" (batch, API only, word timestamps, diarization) and "Voxtral Realtime" (open weights, Apache 2.0, 4B). Both support 13 languages including Spanish. "delivers transcriptions with delay configurable down to sub-200ms"; "At 480ms delay, it stays within 1-2% word error rate" of the offline model. https://mistral.ai/news/voxtral-transcribe-2/
- HF card `mistralai/Voxtral-Mini-4B-Realtime-2602`: 3.4B LM plus 970M audio encoder, delays 80 ms to 2.4 s, recommended 480 ms; FLEURS Spanish WER by delay: 5.34 (160 ms), 4.59 (240), 3.31 (480), 2.98 (960), 2.71 (2400). Inference: vLLM, Transformers 5.2+, community MLX via mlx-audio. No timestamps documented; the vLLM realtime API "currently returns only text" (issue #39735, opened 2026-04-13, open). https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Voxtral-Mini-4B-Realtime-2602 https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/issues/39735

- Fit: a streaming model that is 7x Parakeet's size, slower than every other local option on the leaderboard (RTFx 53 on Spanish), no word timestamps, and no better than Parakeet on Common Voice or MLS Spanish. Not a fit for post-release file transcription.

- Timestamps: Qwen3-ForcedAligner-0.6B, "timestamp prediction for arbitrary units within up to 5 minutes of speech in 11 languages"; the 11 include Spanish (tech report, arXiv 2601.21337). https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21337
