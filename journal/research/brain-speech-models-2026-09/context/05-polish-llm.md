# Polish and summary LLM: model and harness check (2026-09-04)

## Verdict

1. Keep `claude-opus-5-thinking-high` through cursor-agent. Anthropic's placement: "start with Claude Opus 5 for most workloads. Use Claude Fable 5.1 for demanding reasoning and long-horizon agentic work, or when your evals on Claude Opus 5 at higher effort still fall short" (https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/overview). A one-shot transcript rewrite plus summary is neither.
2. "(NO ZDR)" on the Fable ids means Anthropic keeps the prompt (the transcript) and the output for at least 30 days for automated and human harm review "regardless of your Cursor Privacy Mode settings"; Opus 5 is "Zero Data Retention compatible" in Cursor, so the confidential transcript is not stored after the response (https://cursor.com/docs/models/claude-fable-5, https://cursor.com/docs/models/claude-opus-5, https://cursor.com/docs/enterprise/privacy-and-data-governance).
3. Fable 5.1 costs 2x Opus 5 ($10 / $50 vs $5 / $25 per MTok) and Anthropic publishes no multilingual, long-context retrieval, or instruction-following benchmark that would show a gap for Spanish transcript correction; its multilingual performance is "on par with Claude Fable 5" and its knowledge-work eval lead over Opus 5 is 1853 vs 1824 on GDPval-AA v2 (https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing, https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/fable-5-1/whats-new-fable-5-1, https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-1).
4. Change the batch script from `claude-opus-5-thinking-xhigh` to `-thinking-high`. Anthropic documents `xhigh` for "Long-running agentic and coding tasks (over 30 minutes)" and warns that a long single-request deliverable at `xhigh` or `max` may be drafted in thinking and written again, "which means a longer wait and more output tokens"; `high` is the documented starting point (https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/effort, https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-fable-5-1).
5. Test `claude-opus-5-thinking-medium` on a few meetings: on Opus 5 Anthropic says to "use `low` and `medium` liberally as your primary control for token cost and response time wherever your evals show quality holds" (https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/effort). No harness change is needed; `claude -p` is the only alternative that adds something (a separate `--effort` flag and subscription billing), and it does not change the retention picture.

## Notes per question

### 1. Anthropic lineup and positioning

Release dates from the model pages: Opus 5 July 24, 2026 (https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/opus-5/overview); Fable 5 June 9, 2026, now "Legacy" (https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/fable-5/overview); Fable 5.1 September 1, 2026 (https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/fable-5-1/overview); Sonnet 5 June 30, 2026 (https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/sonnet-5/overview). All four: 1M context, 128K max output, default effort `high`, adaptive thinking (always on for Fable). Knowledge cutoffs: Fable 5.1 Jun 2026, Opus 5 May 2026, Sonnet 5 Jan 2026 (https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/overview).

Positioning: the models overview says "If you're unsure which model to use, start with Claude Opus 5 for most workloads. Use Claude Fable 5.1 for demanding reasoning and long-horizon agentic work, or when your evals on Claude Opus 5 at higher effort still fall short." The model selection matrix lists Fable 5.1 for "Agent sessions that run for hours, multistep deep research, analysis carried through to a finished document, spreadsheet, or deck" and Opus 5 for "Complex agentic coding and enterprise work" (https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/choosing-a-model). Fable 5.1's documented gains over Fable 5 are in six areas: agentic coding over long sessions, knowledge work with documents, spreadsheets, and slides, research and search, vision, long-context work, and computer use. The same page says "Multilingual performance is on par with Claude Fable 5" (https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/fable-5-1/whats-new-fable-5-1). Opus 5's capability list includes "Long-context work ... its instruction following, tool calling, and reasoning stay consistent throughout the window" and "Office and document tasks" (https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5).

Anthropic does not position Fable 5.1 as better than Opus 5 for document editing or summarization as such; the Fable 5.1 claim is about carrying analysis through to a finished document in long agentic sessions. The pages do not mention transcript correction, editing, or Spanish.

Benchmarks: the Fable 5.1 announcement publishes Terminal-Bench-Science 0.1 (52.6% vs Opus 5 29.0%), Terminal-Bench 4.0 (55.8% vs 52.3%), GDPval-AA v2 knowledge work (1853 vs 1824), OSWorld 2.0, Humanity's Last Exam (60.9% vs 56.6% no tools), AutomationBench (31.4% vs 26.9%), CursorBench 3.2.0 (73.4% vs 70.0%). No MMMLU, MGSM, MRCR, Graphwalks, IFBench, or IFEval numbers appear (https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-1). The Opus 5 announcement also publishes no multilingual, long-context retrieval, or instruction-following benchmark (https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-5). Nothing published maps to faithful Spanish transcript correction.

### 2. "NO ZDR" in Cursor

Cursor's data-use page: with Privacy Mode on, "Non-ZDR models will be designated as such or require an admin to opt-in to enable the model for your workspace" (https://cursor.com/data-use). The enterprise page names them: "Claude Fable 5.1 and Claude Fable 5 work this way. Anthropic stores their inputs and outputs to run automatic and human harm-prevention reviews. This data is not used for training or product improvement. For Enterprise customers and customers with Privacy Mode enabled, requests to these models fail until the model's data retention policy is approved from the dashboard. Opting in applies to the whole team." (https://cursor.com/docs/enterprise/privacy-and-data-governance). The Fable 5 model page adds: retention happens "regardless of your Cursor Privacy Mode settings" and "Retained customer data is being deleted after 30 days, automatically and permanently, unless subject to a safety investigation or legal hold" (https://cursor.com/docs/models/claude-fable-5). The Fable 5.1 page: "Fable 5.1 is on by default for every individual customer or Team account for which Cursor Privacy Mode is disabled"; Privacy Mode and Enterprise accounts must opt in from the dashboard (https://cursor.com/docs/models/claude-fable-5-1).

What happens to a confidential transcript on a NO ZDR model: the full prompt (transcript plus prompts/polish.md) and the model output are stored by Anthropic for at least 30 days and scanned by automated safety systems, with possible human review; Anthropic's own Covered Models article says the data is deleted after 30 days "unless they are subject to a safety investigation or we are legally required to maintain them" (https://support.claude.com/en/articles/15425695-covered-models). Anthropic's retention page states "Retained data is never used for model training without your express permission" (https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/manage-claude/api-and-data-retention). With Opus 5 under Privacy Mode, Cursor's ZDR agreement applies and Anthropic does not store the transcript after the response, except when a request "trigger[s] abuse detectors" (https://cursor.com/data-use).

Cursor CLI and Privacy Mode: none of the CLI pages (overview, headless, parameters, output-format, using) mention Privacy Mode (https://cursor.com/docs/cli/headless, https://cursor.com/docs/cli/reference/parameters). Privacy Mode is described as an account or team setting enabled "in settings or by a team or enterprise admin" (https://cursor.com/security), so the CLI, which authenticates to the same account, should inherit it; this is inference, not a documented statement. Two documented CLI facts matter here: `--print` "Has access to all tools, including write and shell" (https://cursor.com/docs/cli/reference/parameters), and Cursor's output-format page says "Thinking events are suppressed in print mode and will not appear in any output format" (https://cursor.com/docs/cli/reference/output-format). That statement does not match the saved file: the 433 KB `.reasoning.jsonl` from one meeting contains 1,932 `thinking.delta` events carrying about 15k characters of summarized reasoning plus 129 assistant text events (verified locally 2026-09-04); the file size is per-token JSON envelope overhead.

### 3. Effort levels

Anthropic's effort table: `max` "Absolute maximum capability with no constraints on token spending", for "Tasks requiring the deepest possible reasoning"; `xhigh` "Extended capability for long-horizon work", typical use "Long-running agentic and coding tasks (over 30 minutes) with token budgets in the millions"; `high` "Equivalent to not setting the parameter", for "Complex reasoning, difficult coding problems, agentic tasks"; `medium` "Balanced approach with moderate token savings"; `low` "Most efficient. Significant token savings with some capability reduction" (https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/effort).

Opus 5 guidance on the same page: "Start with `high`, the default, and adjust based on your evals: step up to `xhigh` for demanding coding and agentic work, or to `max` when a task justifies unconstrained token spending, and use `low` and `medium` liberally as your primary control for token cost and response time wherever your evals show quality holds." Also: "Effort controls thinking volume, not visible response length: on Claude Opus 5, changing effort does not reliably shorten responses, so prompt for length instead." The Opus 5 prompting page: "`low` and `medium` effort produce strong quality at a fraction of the tokens and latency of higher settings" (https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5).

Fable 5.1 guidance: "Start with `high`, the default. Step up to `xhigh` or `max` for the most capability-sensitive agentic and coding work, and step down to `medium` or `low` for routine or latency-sensitive work once your evals show quality holds." The prompting page adds a warning that applies directly to a transcript rewrite: "When a single request asks for a long deliverable, such as a full rewrite of a long document, it may draft much of that deliverable in its thinking and then write it out again as the reply, which means a longer wait and more output tokens. The simplest approach is to run requests like these at `high`" (https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-fable-5-1). Claude Code's own table calls `max` "prone to overthinking. Test before adopting broadly" (https://code.claude.com/docs/en/model-config).

Conclusion for this pipeline: `xhigh` is documented for coding and agentic runs, not document editing; `high` is the documented starting point on both Opus 5 and Fable 5.1; `medium` is the documented cost step-down on Opus 5 where quality holds.

Cursor's non-thinking variants (`claude-opus-5-high` without `-thinking-`) most likely map to Anthropic's `thinking: disabled`, which on Opus 5 is allowed only at effort `high` or below and which Anthropic advises against: "for most tasks, thinking enabled at `low` effort performs better than thinking disabled at similar cost" (https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5). Cursor's mapping is not documented; the Cursor Opus 5 page only says the "high thinking variant" is recommended (https://cursor.com/docs/models/claude-opus-5).

### 4. Harnesses for a headless one-shot job

Claude Code: `claude -p "<prompt>" --model claude-opus-5 --effort high --output-format stream-json --permission-mode acceptEdits` runs non-interactively; `--allowedTools` pre-approves tools; `--add-dir` grants extra directories; `--json-schema` gives validated structured output; `--bare` skips hooks, skills, CLAUDE.md, and MCP and "is the recommended mode for scripted and SDK calls", but "bare mode doesn't use your subscription login" and needs `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` (https://code.claude.com/docs/en/headless, https://code.claude.com/docs/en/cli-reference). Without `--bare`, a `-p` run loads the working directory's hooks and `.mcp.json` "even in a folder you've never trusted". `/effort` and `/model` accept an argument inside `-p` prompts from v2.1.205. Billing: on Pro or Max, usage is included in the subscription; Fable "can bill to usage credits" (https://code.claude.com/docs/en/costs, https://code.claude.com/docs/en/model-config). Retention for a Pro or Max login: 30 days when model-improvement is off, 5 years when on, changeable at claude.ai/settings/data-privacy-controls (https://code.claude.com/docs/en/data-usage). Anthropic bars third parties from offering claude.ai login in their products, so a subscription login works only through Claude Code itself (https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-sdk/overview).

opencode: `opencode run --model anthropic/claude-opus-5 --dir <dir> --auto "<prompt>"`, `--format json` for raw events (https://opencode.ai/docs/cli). Model options for Anthropic are documented only as `thinking: {type: "enabled", budgetTokens}` with `high` and `max` variants meaning thinking budgets (https://opencode.ai/docs/models); Anthropic returns a 400 for `budget_tokens` on Opus 5 and Fable models, so effort selection through opencode is unverified. Zen gateway lists Fable 5.1, Fable 5, Opus 5, Sonnet 5 at Anthropic list prices, and notes Anthropic "Requests are retained for 30 days" (https://opencode.ai/docs/zen).

Direct API: `output_config.effort` on `client.messages.create`/`stream` with `model="claude-opus-5"`; thinking is on by default on Opus 5 and always on for Fable; 128K output requires streaming; Fable 5.1 additionally rejects forced `tool_choice`, and prompts and thinking blocks are bound to the producing model (https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/effort, https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/fable-5-1/migration-guide). Retention: 30-day standard for commercial accounts; ZDR by arrangement; Covered Models 400 under ZDR; Batch API 50% off (https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/manage-claude/api-and-data-retention, https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing).

cursor-agent: already in use; see the harness table. The relevant difference from `claude -p` is the effort control surface: cursor-agent bakes effort into the model id, Claude Code exposes `--effort` separately.

### 5. GPT-5.6

OpenAI's model docs list `gpt-5.6-sol` (alias `gpt-5.6`, "Flagship model for complex professional work", $4 / $20 per MTok, cached input $0.40, "Premium rates apply for inputs exceeding 272K tokens", reasoning effort none, low, medium (default), high, xhigh, max), `gpt-5.6-terra` ($2 / $12, "balance intelligence and cost"), `gpt-5.6-luna` ($0.20 / $1.20, "cost-sensitive, high-volume workloads"), all with a 1,050,000-token context (922,000 max input), 128,000 max output, knowledge cutoff February 16, 2026; `gpt-6-astra` ($10 / $50) sits above them (https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models, https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-5.6-sol). Cursor's model list prices Sol at $4 / $20 with "Promotional pricing through November 21, 2026" and Terra as "Mid-tier GPT-5.6 variant between Sol and Luna" (https://cursor.com/docs/models). OpenAI's docs say nothing about long-document summarization or transcript editing; the naming note is that "the number identifies a model's generation, while Sol, Terra, and Luna identify durable capability tiers" (search snippet from https://openai.com/index/previewing-gpt-5-6-sol/, page itself not fetchable).

### 6. Prompting Fable 5.1 vs Opus 5

Anthropic's statement about over-prescriptive prompts is on the Fable 5 prompting page: "Skills developed for prior models are often too prescriptive for Claude Fable 5 and can degrade output quality. Review and consider removing older instructions if default performance is better." and "Instruction-following is improved enough that you can steer most behaviors with a brief instruction rather than enumerating each behavior by name" (https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-fable-5). For Fable 5.1: "Your existing Claude Fable 5 prompts should perform well on Claude Fable 5.1 without changes", with three items relevant to a summary task: "When summarizing documents, Claude Fable 5.1 is more likely than Claude Fable 5 to reproduce passages of the source text without marking them as quotations" (fix: one worked example in the system prompt); prose "denser than Claude Fable 5's: sentences run longer and there are fewer paragraph breaks"; and "Prefer targeted edits over whole-file rewrites" because it "is more likely to rewrite the entire file than make a targeted edit" (https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-fable-5-1, https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/fable-5-1/whats-new-fable-5-1).

For Opus 5: "Claude Opus 5's default user-facing responses run longer than prior Opus models'"; "files that Claude Opus 5 writes to disk (reports, Markdown documents, summaries) are often longer than on prior models", with the suggested line "Match the length of written documents to what the task needs: cover the substance, but do not pad with filler sections, redundant summaries, or boilerplate."; and "If your prompt contains explicit verification instructions ... remove them: instructions like these cause over-verification on Claude Opus 5" (https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5). Those three are the edits worth checking in prompts/polish.md.
