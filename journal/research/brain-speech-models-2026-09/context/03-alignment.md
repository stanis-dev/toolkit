# Word-level timestamp alignment: keep, switch, or drop (as of 2026-09-04)

## Verdict

Keep a forced-alignment stage. No ASR model's native word timestamps match a forced aligner in the primary comparisons: on TIMIT the word boundary error is 19 ms for MFA, 37 ms for MMS-FA, 42 ms for Whisper large-v3 cross-attention DTW, 53 ms for Voxtral and 80 ms for Parakeet TDT durations (Zeyer, Schlüter, Ney, July 2026, https://arxiv.org/html/2607.06831v1), and on meeting audio (AMI) the stock Whisper DTW that mlx-whisper ports reaches F1 47.1 at a 50 ms collar against 63.5 for WhisperX's wav2vec2 alignment (Yeh, Meng, Tang, Sept 2025, https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09987v1).
Switch the aligner, after a side-by-side trial: Qwen3-ForcedAligner-0.6B (Qwen, 2026-01-29, Apache-2.0) is one model for Spanish and English, states that it "supports cross-lingual, code-switched scenarios", reports 36.8 ms (Spanish) and 37.5 ms (English) accumulated average shift against 108.0 ms and 92.1 ms for WhisperX's wav2vec2 aligners on the same MFA-labeled sets (https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21337v1, Table 9), and runs on Apple Silicon through mlx-audio (https://github.com/Blaizzy/mlx-audio/blob/main/mlx_audio/stt/models/qwen3_asr/README.md). Its caveats: 80 ms timestamp grid, 5-minute input limit, ground truth for the published numbers is MFA pseudo-labels, and no third-party benchmark includes it yet.
The fallback that stays inside torchaudio is MMS_FA (one romanized model for 1,130 languages, CC-BY-NC 4.0): 37 ms / 46 ms word boundary error on TIMIT / Buckeye (Zeyer 2026) and 43 ms / 50 ms mean error against 110 ms / 111 ms for WhisperX in the MFA authors' 2026 benchmark (https://arxiv.org/html/2606.18466v1, Table 4). whispermlx 3.13.1 can load it with `--align_model MMS_FA` but does no romanization, so accented Spanish letters fall to its wildcard column (verified locally against the label sets below); it needs an accent-folding patch before it is a fair test.
whispermlx 3.13.1 (2026-08-07) and upstream WhisperX 3.8.6 (2026-05-25) still ship the classic defaults (WAV2VEC2_ASR_BASE_960H for en, VOXPOPULI_ASR_BASE_10K_ES for es) and offer no other aligner (https://github.com/KalebJS/whispermlx, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/m-bain/whisperX/main/whisperx/alignment.py).

## Per-candidate notes

### Current stage: wav2vec2 CTC in whispermlx 3.13.1

- Installed version 3.13.1 in the brain venv; changelog dates it 2026-08-07 (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/KalebJS/whispermlx/main/CHANGELOG.md). Alignment-related changelog entries since 2025 add language models (Basque, Swedish, Indonesian), fix error messages and revert a wildcard change; none adds a new aligner type.
- Defaults in the installed `alignment.py`: `"en": "WAV2VEC2_ASR_BASE_960H"`, `"es": "VOXPOPULI_ASR_BASE_10K_ES"` (same dictionary as upstream https://raw.githubusercontent.com/m-bain/whisperX/main/whisperx/alignment.py). Upstream WhisperX published 3.3.0 through 3.8.6 between 2025-01-02 and 2026-05-25 (https://pypi.org/project/whisperx/#history) without changing these defaults or adding MMS.
- `load_align_model` accepts any name in `torchaudio.pipelines.__all__` or any HF `Wav2Vec2ForCTC`; CLI `--align_model` passes it through (`__main__.py` line 34, `transcribe.py` line 172). Alignment runs with torch on the `device` you pass; README: "Controls VAD/alignment/diarization only; MLX inference auto-uses GPU" (https://github.com/KalebJS/whispermlx).
- `asr.py` line 133 sets `word_timestamps=False` on mlx-whisper, so Whisper's own DTW timestamps are never used.
- Frame resolution: wav2vec2 emits one frame per 20 ms (Rousso et al. 2024, section 2.2, https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19363).
- Code-switching today: the Spanish label set is `- | e a o s n r i l d c t u p m b q y g v h ó f í á j z ñ é x ú k w ü` (verified with `torchaudio.pipelines.VOXPOPULI_ASR_BASE_10K_ES.get_labels()` locally), so English words are alignable letter-by-letter but scored by a Spanish acoustic model. Characters outside the set go to a wildcard column (`alignment.py` "Build tokens, mapping unknown chars to a wildcard column").
- Accuracy of this family in primary comparisons: Rousso 2024, TIMIT word level, end timestamps: WhisperX 22.4 / 52.7 / 82.4 / 94.2 percent within 10 / 25 / 50 / 100 ms, MFA 41.6 / 72.8 / 89.4 / 97.4; Buckeye (500 ms outlier cut) WhisperX 22.8 / 52.3 / 81.8 / 93.9. McAuliffe et al. 2026, start timestamps: WhisperX TIMIT mean 110.04 ms, 15.55% within 50 ms; Buckeye mean 110.90 ms (https://arxiv.org/html/2606.18466v1). The two papers disagree by a factor of three on WhisperX; the 2026 paper does not name the checkpoint and notes the start-vs-end convention difference. Qwen report: WhisperX Spanish AAS 108.0 ms on raw utterances, 4549.9 ms on 300 s concatenations (https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21337v1 Table 9); the long-form number reflects aligning whole 5-minute inputs at once, which whispermlx avoids by aligning per Whisper segment.
- torchaudio status: whispermlx pins `torch~=2.8.0`, `torchaudio~=2.8.0` (installed 2.8.0). torchaudio entered a maintenance phase in 2.8 (2025-08-06); `forced_align`, `RNNTLoss`, `lfilter` were kept after user feedback in 2.10 (2026-01-21), and "most of the APIs in transforms, functional, compliance.kaldi, models and pipelines modules will remain" (https://github.com/pytorch/audio/issues/3902; dates from https://pypi.org/project/torchaudio/#history).

### MMS_FA (torchaudio) and ctc-forced-aligner

- torchaudio `MMS_FA`: "Trained on 31K hours of data in 1,130 languages", CC-BY-NC 4.0, no word-boundary token (https://docs.pytorch.org/audio/stable/generated/torchaudio.pipelines.MMS_FA.html). Labels verified locally: `- a i e n o u t s r m k l d g h y b p w c v j z f ' q x *` (the `*` is the star token added by `get_model()`, disable with `with_star=False`). The multilingual tutorial romanizes text with uroman before alignment and derives word boundaries by splitting the transcript (https://docs.pytorch.org/audio/stable/tutorials/forced_alignment_for_multilingual_data_tutorial.html).
- The MMS paper explains the star token: "we introduce a star token (⟨∗⟩; ...) to which audio segments can be mapped if there is no good alternative in the text", with digits replaced by it and its posterior set to one; alignment posteriors are computed on 15-second chunks and concatenated (https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13516, section 3.1.3).
- Consequence for whispermlx: `MMS_FA` is in `torchaudio.pipelines.__all__`, so `--align_model MMS_FA` loads; blank id 0 matches label `-`. But whispermlx never romanizes, so `á é í ó ú ñ ü` are not in the dictionary and get the wildcard column, and spaces are dropped rather than mapped to `|`. A pre-alignment accent-folding step (or uroman) is needed for Spanish.
- Accuracy: Zeyer 2026 Table I, native CTC posteriors: MMS-FA 37 ms TIMIT, 46 ms Buckeye WBE (MFA 19 / 32) (https://arxiv.org/html/2607.06831v1). McAuliffe 2026 Table 4: MMS mean 43.06 ms TIMIT, 13.05 / 26.52 / 63.73 / 95.91 percent within 10 / 25 / 50 / 100 ms; Buckeye mean 49.54 ms, 61.85% within 50 ms. Rousso 2024: MMS 18.6 / 43.5 / 75.7 / 94.7 on TIMIT, median 29.3 ms; on TIMIT MMS trails the English wav2vec2 aligner at 50 ms (75.7 vs 82.4) while the 2026 benchmark has MMS far ahead (63.73 vs 15.55).
- ctc-forced-aligner 1.0.2 (2025-02-09) wraps the same checkpoint (`MahmoudAshraf/mms-300m-1130-forced-aligner`, a torchaudio-to-transformers conversion, CC-BY-NC 4.0), requires `--romanize` with the default model, and offers ONNX Runtime CPU/GPU or torch backends; README lists `cuda` or `cpu`, no MPS or MLX (https://github.com/MahmoudAshraf97/ctc-forced-aligner, https://pypi.org/project/ctc-forced-aligner/, https://huggingface.co/MahmoudAshraf/mms-300m-1130-forced-aligner).
- A June 2026 paper (Weber, Zehavi, Rousso, Keshet) trains a learned dynamic-programming aligner on MMS representations that "outperforms Montreal Forced Aligner (MFA) and MMS-based alignment" and generalizes to unseen languages; no Spanish evaluation and no release information visible on the abstract page (https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10675).

### NeMo Forced Aligner

- "NFA is a tool for generating token-, word- and segment-level timestamps of speech in audio using NeMo's CTC-based Automatic Speech Recognition models"; CTC or hybrid models in CTC mode only; CPU or GPU via `torch.device` strings (https://docs.nvidia.com/nemo-framework/user-guide/latest/nemotoolkit/tools/nemo_forced_aligner.html).
- The 2023 paper measured precision/recall on AMI at a 200 ms collar (NFA 98.35 / 98.35 with ground-truth text; WhisperX v3 90.26 / 70.12 with ASR text) (https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2023/rastorgueva23_interspeech.pdf). Tighter benchmarks: TIMIT mean 78.24 ms, Buckeye 88.62 ms (McAuliffe 2026 Table 4); Spanish AAS 124.7 ms raw, 240.2 ms at 300 s (Qwen report Table 9). The LLM-ForcedAligner paper attributes the drift to CTC peaks lagging speech onset (https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18220v1).
- Spanish-specific: NeMo issue 14854 (opened 2025-10-01, still open) reports `RNNTBPEDecoding.get_words_offsets()` failing on `¡Hola!` because it "assumes a punctuation always comes after some words" (https://github.com/NVIDIA-NeMo/Speech/issues/14854).

### Qwen3-ForcedAligner-0.6B

- Card: 11 languages "Chinese, English, Cantonese, French, German, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese, Russian, Spanish", "up to 5 minutes of speech", Apache-2.0, 0.9B params on disk; usage via `from qwen_asr import Qwen3ForcedAligner` (https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-ForcedAligner-0.6B). Released 2026-01-29; transformers-native support 2026-06-26 via `Qwen/Qwen3-ForcedAligner-0.6B-hf` with `AutoModelForTokenClassification` (https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-ASR, https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-ForcedAligner-0.6B-hf).
- Method: non-autoregressive timestamp prediction from an 80 ms audio encoder frame rate; "the special token [time] and the timestamp prediction layer do not rely on language-specific phoneme sets or dictionaries" (https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21337v1; https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18220v1). The inference code multiplies output ids by `timestamp_segment_time` from the model config; for non-CJK languages words are `text.split()` tokens after `clean_token` (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-ASR/main/qwen_asr/inference/qwen3_forced_aligner.py).
- Accuracy (Table 9, AAS in ms, lower is better): MFA-labeled raw: Spanish 36.8 (NFA 124.7, WhisperX 108.0), English 37.5 (NFA 107.5, WhisperX 92.1), 10-language average 42.9 (NFA 129.8, WhisperX 133.2). 300 s concatenations: Spanish 39.6 (NFA 240.2, WhisperX 4549.9), English 58.6 (NFA 226.7, WhisperX 227.2), "Cross-lingual 34.2". Human-labeled (Chinese only): raw 27.8, noisy 41.8, 300 s 24.8, "Concat-Cross-lingual 42.5". Training labels are MFA pseudo-labels: "we use pseudo-timestamp labels generated by the Montreal forced aligner (MFA)", so the MFA-labeled rows measure agreement with MFA, and the authors state the limitation that human labels cover only Chinese (https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18220v1).
- Code-switching: report text: "Qwen3-ForcedAligner-0.6B covers multiple languages with a single model and supports cross-lingual, code-switched scenarios" (https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21337v1, section 4.6). The API still takes one `language` string per input; mlx-audio: "A single language is applied to every input" (https://github.com/Blaizzy/mlx-audio/tree/main/mlx_audio/stt/models/qwen3_asr).
- Apple Silicon: mlx-audio ships `mlx-community/Qwen3-ForcedAligner-0.6B-8bit` (1.27 GB, converted with mlx-audio 0.3.1) with `model.generate(audio=..., text=..., language="English")` returning `ForcedAlignItem(text, start_time, end_time)` (https://github.com/Blaizzy/mlx-audio/blob/main/mlx_audio/stt/models/qwen3_asr/README.md, https://huggingface.co/mlx-community/Qwen3-ForcedAligner-0.6B-8bit). mlx-audio latest is 0.5.1, 2026-08-31 (https://pypi.org/project/mlx-audio/). The official `qwen_asr` code falls back to `torch.device('cpu')` when no CUDA device; the HF card examples use `device_map="cuda:0"`.
- Fit with the pipeline: whispermlx already aligns per Whisper segment with audio slices, so the 5-minute cap is not binding if the aligner is called per segment or per group of segments; what changes is the 80 ms grid (wav2vec2: 20 ms) and dropping torchaudio for this stage.

### Parakeet-TDT-0.6B-v3 and parakeet-mlx

- Card: 25 languages including Spanish and English, CC-BY-4.0, "word-level and segment-level timestamps" via `transcribe(..., timestamps=True)` in NeMo or `processor.decode(..., durations=output.durations)` in transformers; no timestamp accuracy claim and no code-switching statement (https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3). Encoder frames are 80 ms after 8x subsampling (https://arxiv.org/html/2509.14128).
- parakeet-mlx 0.5.2 (2026-06-05) defaults to `mlx-community/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3` and exposes `AlignedToken(text, start, end, duration)`; source: `start=hypothesis.step * self.time_ratio`, `duration=duration * self.time_ratio` where `time_ratio = subsampling_factor / sample_rate * hop_length` (= 8 × 160 / 16000 = 0.08 s) (https://github.com/senstella/parakeet-mlx, `parakeet_mlx/parakeet.py` on branch `master`; https://pypi.org/project/parakeet-mlx/). So word starts are quantized to 80 ms and the "duration" is the TDT skip, not an acoustic offset.
- Accuracy: Zeyer 2026 Table I, native posteriors: Parakeet TDT 80 ms TIMIT / 90 ms Buckeye; Parakeet RNN-T 79 / 93; Parakeet CTC 77 / 99; MMS-FA 37 / 46; MFA 19 / 32 (https://arxiv.org/html/2607.06831v1). AssemblyAI 2024 found Parakeet RNN-T ahead of Parakeet CTC on timestamps and needed a constant -65 ms bias correction on its own RNN-T; results are a figure, no numbers in text (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.09841, section 4.7).

### Canary-1B-v2

- 25 languages incl. es, en; "Accurate word-level and segment-level timestamps" but produced by NFA with "an additional ASR model, a Parakeet architecture model with CTC decoder comprising 600M parameters"; the card says you can drop the auxiliary CTC model if timestamps are not needed (https://huggingface.co/nvidia/canary-1b-v2, https://arxiv.org/html/2509.14128). This is forced alignment under another name, at NFA accuracy. The paper says the unified tokenizer "enables natural code-switching functionality". No MLX port found; parakeet-mlx supports TDT, RNNT, CTC and TDTCTC Parakeet variants only.

### Whisper cross-attention DTW (mlx-whisper) and CrisperWhisper

- mlx-whisper `transcribe(..., word_timestamps=True)` (https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-examples/tree/main/whisper); `timing.py` runs DTW over cross-attention weights of `alignment_heads` with a width-7 median filter on 20 ms frames (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ml-explore/mlx-examples/main/whisper/mlx_whisper/timing.py).
- Accuracy: Yeh 2025 (Whisper medium): official approach F1@50 ms 64.0 TIMIT, 62.5 LibriSpeech, 47.1 AMI versus WhisperX 79.9 / 79.5 / 63.5 and MFA 91.0 on TIMIT; their head-selection plus character tokenization reaches 80.7 / 80.6 / 61.9, English only, code at https://github.com/30stomercury/whisper-char-alignment (https://arxiv.org/html/2509.09987v1). Zeyer 2026: Whisper-large-v3 cross-attention 42 ms TIMIT / 49 ms Buckeye WBE, Whisper-base 51 / 62. CrisperWhisper card: large-v3 F1 0.66 on AMI IHM and 0.48 on Common Voice versus CrisperWhisper 0.79 / 0.80 (https://huggingface.co/nyrahealth/CrisperWhisper); CrisperWhisper paper Table 1 (synthetic, 0.2 s collar): WhisperT 74.7 F1, WhisperX 76.7, CrisperWhisper 84.7; noisy 68.3 / 59.0 / 79.5 (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.16589).
- CrisperWhisper is out for this pipeline: "trained on a mixture of english and german datasets so we only gurantee good performance on these languages", CC-BY-NC 4.0.

### Voxtral Transcribe 2

- Two models released 2026-02-04: Voxtral Mini Transcribe V2 (API only, $0.003/min, "Generate precise start and end timestamps for each word") and Voxtral-Mini-4B-Realtime-2602 (Apache-2.0 open weights, 13 languages incl. es, en) (https://mistral.ai/news/voxtral-transcribe-2/, https://docs.mistral.ai/models/voxtral-mini-transcribe-26-02). The open realtime card lists no timestamp feature; on HF discussion 39 Mistral's Sanchit Gandhi answers "You can use the position of the `[STREAMING_WORD]` token as a proxy for the end timestamp of an emission group" (https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Voxtral-Mini-4B-Realtime-2602/discussions/39). Zeyer 2026 measured Voxtral self-attention alignment at 53 / 52 ms WBE.

### Qwen3-ASR

- Qwen3-ASR-1.7B / 0.6B: 52 languages, no native timestamps; README: "If you want to return timestamps, pass `forced_aligner` and its init kwargs" (https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-ASR).

### ElevenLabs Scribe v2 (cloud)

- "Precise word-level timestamps", 90+ languages, diarization up to 32 speakers, `timestamps_granularity` `word` or `character`, `words[]` with `start`, `end`, `speaker_id`, `logprob`, plus `language_probability`; "automatic language recognition" for the realtime model (https://elevenlabs.io/docs/capabilities/speech-to-text, https://elevenlabs.io/docs/api-reference/speech-to-text/convert, https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/models). No timestamp accuracy figures on the official pages.

### Cross-cutting evidence on native timestamps vs forced alignment

- Zeyer, Schlüter, Ney (2026-07-07): on TIMIT / Buckeye, every native ASR alignment is worse than MFA (19 / 32 ms): CrisperWhisper 33 / 47, MMS-FA 37 / 46, Whisper-large-v3 42 / 49, Voxtral 53 / 52, Parakeet TDT 80 / 90, Canary-Qwen 131 / 211; "A strong native aligner is usually still somewhat better, and the gradient is considerably more expensive" (https://arxiv.org/html/2607.06831v1).
- McAuliffe, Gunter, Wagner, Sonderegger (2026-06-16): "neural alignment systems struggle to match the performance of HMM-GMM systems"; ASR-based aligners leave gaps between words, so start vs end timestamp choice changes scores (https://arxiv.org/html/2606.18466v1).
- Rousso, Cohen, Keshet, Chodroff (Interspeech 2024): "The MFA outperformed both WhisperX and MMS" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19363).

## What I could not verify

- Any third-party measurement of Qwen3-ForcedAligner on Spanish or English against human labels; all published Spanish/English numbers use MFA pseudo-labels from the model's own authors.
- Qwen3-ForcedAligner behavior on a transcript that mixes Spanish and English words inside one `language="Spanish"` call; the report claims code-switched support but shows only a "Cross-lingual" aggregate row without a description of that test set.
- Runtime of Qwen3-ForcedAligner in mlx-audio on an M4 Max (no numbers published), and whether mlx-audio chunks inputs longer than 5 minutes for the aligner (README mentions chunk batching without detail).
